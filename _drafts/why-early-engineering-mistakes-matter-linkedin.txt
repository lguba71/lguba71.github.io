What is one early engineering mistake that taught you something useful?

Nobody starts by running fast.

First we learn to stand, walk, fall, stand up, and try again.

Engineering experience is similar.

Earlier in my career, I learned that moving fast is not always the same as making good progress.

Sometimes the mistake is not a wrong calculation.

Sometimes it is accepting the first understanding of the problem too easily.

What did I assume?

What did I not ask?

What constraint did I notice too late?

Who would be affected by the decision later?

Early mistakes are uncomfortable.

But if they improve judgement, they are not wasted.
